Before You Scroll

Why There Is No Rate Sheet Here

Because a rate sheet would be fiction. Terms on investment property financing move with the structure of the deal: the property, the plan, the timeline, the exit, and the experience behind it. Two investors buying identical houses can land in genuinely different places, and any lender publishing one number for all of them is either guessing or hoping you do not read the fine print later.

So this page does something more useful. Each program below tells you who it is for, what problem it solves, what the exit looks like, and, just as importantly, when it is the wrong tool. That is the information that decides whether your deal works. Price is a conversation we have once we know which structure you are actually in.

Call with a property address and a plan and you will get real numbers on your real deal, usually in the same conversation. If the answer is that we are not the right fit, you will get that in the same conversation too.

Buy, Renovate, Sell

Fix and Flip

You found a distressed three bedroom, you have a contractor lined up, and the seller wants to close in twelve days. Fix and flip financing covers the purchase and the renovation together, so you are not scrambling to fund the rehab out of pocket after closing drains your reserves.

The exit is a sale. That means the numbers that matter are the purchase price, the honest cost of the work, and a resale figure that holds up against what has actually sold nearby in the last few months. We would rather challenge an optimistic resale number now than watch you carry the property for six extra months later.

Strong fit
  • A defined, priced scope of work
  • A resale number backed by real comparable sales
  • A contractor identified, not hypothetical
  • Reserves to carry the property through the work
Probably not a fit
  • No scope, no bids, and a rough guess at costs
  • A resale figure that requires the best sale on the street
  • Zero cash left after the down payment
  • A property you intend to live in
Timing, Not Merit

Bridge Loans

Sometimes the deal is fine and the calendar is the problem. A seller will not wait sixty days. Your bank financing is real but slow. You need to close on this property before the sale of another one funds. Bridge capital closes on the strength of the asset now and gets paid off when the permanent financing or the sale lands.

The entire value of a bridge loan is the thing on the other side of it. Short term money is the most expensive money in real estate, and it is worth every dollar when it saves a deal and a disaster when it is used to postpone a decision. We will ask hard questions about your exit for exactly that reason.

Strong fit
  • A specific, dated exit you can describe in one sentence
  • An asset that stands on its own value
  • A closing timeline no conventional lender can meet
  • Permanent financing already in motion
Probably not a fit
  • Hoping an exit appears during the term
  • Using short term debt to cover a shortfall elsewhere
  • A refinance you have not confirmed you will qualify for
Lot to Certificate of Occupancy

Ground-Up Construction

Financing a build from bare land through completion, released in draws as the work gets done. Unlimited draws are available across the project, requested through an online platform, and funds are typically released within 24 hours after approval.

That last detail matters more than most borrowers expect until they live it. A build runs on trades showing up in sequence, and a crew that goes a week unpaid goes to somebody else's job. Draw speed is not a convenience feature on a construction loan. It is the difference between finishing on schedule and finishing whenever the subs come back.

Strong fit
  • Entitled land with permits issued or in motion
  • A builder with completed projects behind them
  • A real budget and a real draw schedule
  • A defined exit, whether sale or refinance to hold
Probably not a fit
  • Raw land with no entitlement path
  • No builder selected and no construction experience
  • A budget assembled from online estimates
Qualified on the Property

DSCR Rental Loans

Long term financing qualified on what the property earns rather than what your tax return says you earn. If you are self-employed, write off aggressively, or have income that is entirely real but a nightmare to document, this is usually the program that solves it.

The underwriting question is simple: does the rent cover the debt? Get that right and the file moves. It is the standard structure for investors building a rental portfolio, and it is why the second, fifth, and tenth property do not get progressively harder to finance the way conventional lending makes them.

Strong fit
  • A rental that covers its own debt service
  • Self-employed or hard-to-document income
  • A buy-and-hold plan, not a quick resale
  • An investor already at the conventional financing limit
Probably not a fit
  • A property that will not rent for enough to cover itself
  • A house needing substantial work before it can be leased
  • A property you or your family will occupy
Nightly Revenue

Short Term Rentals

Financing for properties operating on nightly platforms, underwritten with short term revenue in mind. Forcing a performing nightly rental into a long term lease comparison understates it badly, and that mismatch is what gets these files declined elsewhere.

The two things we look at hardest are revenue evidence and regulatory risk. A documented booking history is the strongest case you can bring. Just as important is what the local ordinance actually permits, because a market that changes its rules mid-hold turns a good asset into a problem overnight.

Strong fit
  • Documented nightly revenue history
  • A market with verifiable, year-round demand
  • Local rules that clearly permit the use
  • A property already furnished and operating
Probably not a fit
  • Projected revenue with nothing behind it
  • A jurisdiction actively restricting nightly rentals
  • Numbers that only work in peak season
Thirty Days and Longer

Mid Term Rentals

Furnished stays of thirty days and up: traveling medical staff, corporate relocations, insurance placements, families between homes. It is a genuinely good asset class sitting in a gap, because it is neither a nightly rental nor a twelve month lease and most lenders have no box for it.

We do. The economics tend to be steadier than nightly rentals with less turnover and less regulatory exposure, while still outperforming a standard lease. If you have been told your mid term rental is unfinanceable, it was a box problem, not a deal problem.

Strong fit
  • Proximity to hospitals, campuses, or corporate corridors
  • Furnished units with placement demand you can evidence
  • An operator who understands the tenant profile
  • A market where standard lease comps understate the asset
Probably not a fit
  • A location with no identifiable source of demand
  • An unfurnished property with no plan to furnish it
  • A concept with no comparable operating properties nearby
Put Equity Back to Work

Cash-Out Refinance

You own a property with real equity in it. That equity is doing nothing for your returns while it sits inside a wall. A cash-out refinance releases it so it can become the down payment on the next deal, the rehab budget on the one you just bought, or the reserves that let you move quickly on the one after that.

This is one of the most useful tools available to an investor and one of the easiest to misuse. The question we will ask is what the money is for. Equity redeployed into a deal with a return is growth. Equity pulled out to service other debt is usually a signal to solve a different problem first, and we will say so.

Strong fit
  • Meaningful equity in a stabilized property
  • A specific, identified use for the proceeds
  • A property that still performs after the new debt
  • An investor scaling into the next acquisition
Probably not a fit
  • Pulling cash with no plan for where it goes
  • New debt the property cannot comfortably carry
  • Covering shortfalls elsewhere in the portfolio
Many Doors, One Loan

Rental Portfolio Loans

You have three, eight, or twenty rentals, each with its own note, its own servicer, its own maturity date, and its own annoying quirk. A portfolio loan consolidates them into one financing position with one closing and one payment.

Beyond the administrative relief, it usually improves how the whole portfolio is treated. Properties are evaluated together rather than one at a time, so a strong performer can carry a softer one, and adding the next acquisition stops being a fresh negotiation every single time.

Strong fit
  • Several stabilized, leased rentals
  • A stack of separate notes you are tired of managing
  • An operator planning to keep acquiring
  • Properties held in an entity rather than personally
Probably not a fit
  • Properties still vacant or mid-renovation
  • A plan to sell individual properties in the near term
  • One or two doors, where individual loans are simpler
Five Units and Up

Multifamily Bridge Loans

Acquisition and repositioning capital for five units and above, sized around the business plan you are executing rather than the rent roll you inherit on day one. That distinction is the whole point of bridge debt on multifamily: permanent lenders price the building as it is, and you are buying it for what it becomes.

Underwriting focuses on the plan. What are the units renting for now, what should they rent for, what does it cost to get there, how long does it take, and who has done this before. A credible answer to those five questions is worth more than a perfect current-year statement.

Strong fit
  • A value-add plan with costed, dated steps
  • Rents demonstrably below market for the submarket
  • An operator who has completed a reposition before
  • A refinance or sale exit identified up front
Probably not a fit
  • A stabilized building with no value to add
  • A plan that depends on rent growth rather than work
  • A first multifamily deal with no operating partner
Commercial and Mixed Use

Commercial Bridge Loans

Short term financing on commercial and mixed use assets while you stabilize occupancy, finish a lease-up, complete a repositioning, or line up permanent debt that is not available to you at today's numbers.

Commercial assets swing harder than residential. One tenant signing or leaving can move the value of the building substantially, which is what makes bridge debt useful here and what makes the exit worth scrutinizing. We want to see the path to stabilization, with dates on it.

Strong fit
  • A credible, dated path to stabilization
  • Leasing activity underway or letters of intent in hand
  • A sponsor with relevant asset-class experience
  • A permanent debt or sale exit already scoped
Probably not a fit
  • An empty building with no leasing plan
  • A stabilization timeline that keeps moving
  • An asset class nobody on the deal has operated
